# Orca · Voice Sheet

One page for anyone writing for Orca Keel, Daybook, or the studio. Draft 0.1, October 21, 2026.

## The posture in one line

Written for the family, with the details one layer down for whoever runs the house's IT. Plain words, short sentences, the confidence to say what it will not do.

## Six rules

1. **Subject, verb, object.** One idea per sentence. "It keeps the record." Not "Orca Keel is designed to intelligently maintain a comprehensive record."
2. **Name the thing.** The water bill, the school form, the return window, the backup drive. Never "tasks," "items," "workflows," "content."
3. **Limits are features.** State what it will not do with the same confidence as what it will. "Nothing listens until you press the button."
4. **Numbers as numbers.** "fifteen minutes," "$138.40," "up to $140, before Friday." Never "lightning fast" or "in seconds."
5. **No exclamation marks, no jokes in the product.** A little dryness in marketing is fine. Warmth comes from specificity, not from cheer.
6. **"You" is the household; "we" is the studio.** "Your record." "We made it this way because." Orca Keel is "it," never "I" or "your assistant."

## Words

| We say | We never say |
|---|---|
| the household computer | the agent computer, smart home hub, AI appliance |
| it keeps the record | AI-powered household intelligence |
| it does only what you said | autonomous, proactive, agentic |
| a grant | a permission, an approval, access |
| a routine | an agent, a bot, a skill, an assistant |
| a matter | a task, a to-do, an item |
| the record, your record | your data, the cloud, your workspace |
| plain files, yours to take | export, sync, seamless |
| nothing listens until you press the button | always-on, hands-free, voice-first |
| it keeps working when the internet is out | offline mode, resilient, edge |
| the box | the device, the unit, the hub, the node |
| the card | the quick-start guide, the manual |
| Daybook | the app, the dashboard, the portal |
| made by Orca | powered by, brought to you by |
| set up from your phone in fifteen minutes | plug and play, zero config, frictionless |

Banned outright in family-facing copy: AI-powered, smart, seamless, intelligent, assistant, agent, solution, ecosystem, leverage, empower, journey, experience, reimagined, next-generation, effortless.

Allowed in the installer's layer, used precisely: authority, grant, ledger, routine, tailnet, signed update, rollback, threat model, open source.

## Sample copy

**Landing page hero**
A quiet computer that keeps the house in order.
Orca Keel runs your household's paperwork: returns, renewals, school forms, repairs, the bill that is due Friday. It keeps the record, does only what you told it to, and keeps working when the internet is out.

**The three claims**
It keeps the record. Every obligation becomes a matter with a deadline, a source and the evidence. The record is plain files on a drive you own. It works without any AI provider at all.
It does only what you said. Nothing happens in your name without a specific grant: this bill, this amount, this week. A routine cannot give itself more. Every action traces back to the grant that allowed it.
It keeps working. Reminders, filing and deadlines run on the box, not in the cloud. A power cut, a bad update or a provider outage does not lose a thing.

**The installer's layer**
Not an agent with your passwords. The other boxes ship a general agent with a shell, a browser and your logins, then put a fence around it. Orca Keel has no shell for routines, no third-party skill store, and no microphone that listens on its own. No terminal, ever. No skill store at launch. Updates that can't brick it. No public ports; pair from your phone. Your record is yours to take.

**The carton**
Front: The household computer. Orca Keel. A quiet computer that keeps the house in order.
Back: In the carton: the box, the power cord, one card, your backup drive. Nothing to assemble. Made by Orca.

**The card**
Three steps. About fifteen minutes.

1. Plug in the power and the Ethernet cable. Wait for the small amber light to blink once.
2. On your phone, open [APP LINK] and enter the code below.
3. Name your household and add the people in it. Daybook takes it from there.
Keep the backup drive in another room. It is how you get everything back.

**Daybook, a matter**
Water bill, October. Due Friday, October 24. Source: a letter photographed Tuesday. Amount: $138.40. Same as last month within a dollar.

**Daybook, a grant**
Pay the water bill, up to $140, before Friday. Nothing else. Granted by Sam, Wednesday 8:12 pm. Expires Friday.

**A reminder on the phone**
The school form is due Thursday. The signed copy is in Daybook; it needs to go in the backpack.

**When it cannot do something**
That needs a grant it doesn't have. Here is what it would do and what it would cost; say yes and it will.

**When something went wrong**
The update on Tuesday failed and the box went back to the version before it. Nothing was lost. It will try again tonight.

**Footer / about**
Orca Keel is made by Orca, a software studio. It started with a spreadsheet.
About the name: Orca is short for "ninjas of orca," an anagram of the founder's name. It stuck.

## Tone by surface

| Surface | Tone |
|---|---|
| Carton, card | Fewest words. Instructions, not persuasion. |
| Landing page, family sections | Plain and warm; the specific thing named every time. |
| Landing page, installer section | Plain and exact; technical words used correctly, never as decoration. |
| Daybook | The record's voice: factual, dated, sourced. Reads like a good clerk's entry. |
| Reminders and notifications | One sentence of what, one of where or what's next. Never a nudge, never an emoji. |
| Errors and limits | Say what happened, what was kept safe, what happens next. |
| Pitch and investors | Same voice; add the numbers and the head-to-head. The tone does not change for money. |
