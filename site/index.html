The household computer

A household has a lot to remember. It doesn’t all have to be you.

Orca Keel is a quiet computer for the work of running a home. Letters, bills, repairs and school forms become a record the household can keep up with, on a box you own.

Set up from your phone. About fifteen minutes.

How it works

From “don’t forget” to a clear next step.

The letter on the counter. The email with a deadline. The receipt you’ll need later. Keep the source, the deadline and the next step together.

01

Bring it into Daybook.

Photograph a letter, forward an email, or press the button and say it. Keep the source with the thing it’s about.

02

Know what needs you.

A bill, a repair or a school form becomes a matter: what it is, when it’s due, and what still needs to happen.

03

Set the limits.

When something needs doing in your name, you give a specific grant. This bill. Up to this amount. Before this day. Nothing else.

For the household

The details stay together. The whole house can keep up.

Daybook is what you open on your phone: the household’s calendar, reminders, notes and ideas, kept with the paperwork behind them.

Take the water bill. The photographed letter, the amount, the deadline and the instruction to pay belong to the same matter. You can see what’s needed and exactly what you allowed.

The record lives in plain files on your own drive. It remains yours to keep, back up and take with you.

The model helps.
The record belongs to you.

Reading a letter or drafting a reply takes a model. Keel works with Claude, ChatGPT or another provider you pick, sending only the part of a matter needed for that job, under a grant. The record, deadlines and reminders don’t need a model. No model runs on the box.

For the installer

Useful enough to help. Limited enough to trust.

A household computer should earn its place in the house. Keel is being built around a record you own, work you can trace, and limits a routine cannot change for itself.

Not an agent with your passwords. No shell for routines. No third-party skill store at launch. No microphone that listens on its own.

  • A grant for the job, not the whole house.

    Every action traces back to the grant that allowed it. A routine cannot give itself more authority.

  • The essentials stay on the box.

    Reminders, filing and deadlines run locally. An internet or model-provider outage does not switch them off.

  • A way back is part of the design.

    A backup drive for your record. Updates designed to roll back if they fail. Plain files you can take with you.

  • Quiet, until you ask.

    Nothing listens until you press the button. Pair from your phone, with no public ports and no terminal to manage.

Diamond Dog is the layer beneath Keel that makes these limits hold: a specific job, clear authority and a record of what happened, including what is still unknown.
